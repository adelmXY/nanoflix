This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
Show everything at once, small, in order. The work is laid out like a photographer's contact sheet or an archive drawer: dozens of small frames in strict rows, with all text set at one small size. Nothing is featured, which is the point: the volume and consistency of the body of work is the statement, and the visitor does the choosing. It suits people and studios whose strength is range and output.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
The name, or one sentence about the work, sits at display size (6–9vw) across the top of the first screen, and the sheet begins directly under it, still inside the first screen. The first screen opens with three or four short blocks of small text across the top on a 12-column grid (who, what, where to reach them), all at 14–16px, and immediately below, the sheet begins: rows of thumbnails at a fixed height (90–140px) with their natural widths, 6–10px gaps, each project forming a run of four to eight frames preceded by its title in the same small text. The sheet fills the whole page width edge to edge with 16–20px margins. Hovering a run dims the others and shows that project's one-line description in a fixed position. A single enlarged frame may interrupt the sheet once per screen at four times the size.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. One face. The name at display size on the first screen; everything else at one text size and one small size; hierarchy below the name comes from position and from a single brighter ink for titles.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Black or white ground, so the thumbnails are the only colour.

## 5. Imagery
**The project has no photographs of its own yet.** First choice: **real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections**: its city and street in old postcards and photographs, an old map of the neighbourhood, its materials up close, its trade in engravings, museum objects of its kind (references/imagery.md §3). Start with `node /workspace/nanoflix/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "culture"`, then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history). Download what you use into the project with `--get` and credit every one on the page (`--credits` prints the footer line). They show the world around the client, never the client's own room, dish, product, person or team. Second, for what no honest picture exists of: draw the subject's own things in code, large, in one flat graphic language and the palette's real colours (its tiles, its windows, its objects; references/imagery.md §8). Third: the dealt photographs, only where an honestly generic picture fits (the sea, the weather, a material), never one a visitor could take for the thing itself and never an unrelated scene. One treatment for all of them. If this direction is built on photographs, plan the set before building (four to six pictures of one family) and reuse them at different crops and scales instead of finding or drawing a new picture for every slot; keep drawings as `<symbol>` + `<use>` or generate them with a small script so the page stays light. List every found picture (with its source and licence), every dealt photograph and every drawing that stands in for photography in your report.

Many images: at least 24 frames to make a sheet. They can be project photographs from the dealt set cropped to mixed ratios, or page captures of the work. Square corners, no captions on the frames.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Text band, then sheet
A black or white field on a seven-column grid with 14px margins and 14px gaps. There is no display type: everything is set at one size, 16px, in one weight. The top band is plain text hanging from a line 20px below the top edge: the name in column one; one sentence on availability in column two; a list of five or six services from column three; a longer list of clients or awards from column five; and two links, profile and the single contact action, at the right end of column seven. Lists run one item per line. Below the lists about 300px of ground is left empty. At mid-height the sheet begins: one thumbnail per column, each the full column width (about 190px) at its natural ratio, top-aligned, with the project title in the text size above the first frame of each run of two to four frames. Rows continue below the fold with 40px between them. Frames have square corners and sit directly on the ground with no border and no caption on the image.

On a phone: The grid becomes two columns with 12px gaps. The name and the availability sentence take the top; services and clients sit side by side under them as two short lists at 15px. An 80px gap follows, then the sheet: two frames per row at natural ratios, each project title above its first frame. The contact action is a text link fixed bottom-right.
Headline: None at display size: the name is set at 16px with the rest of the text, and one sentence of up to 60 characters says what the person does.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names colours (black, white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. gallery — Dense masonry sheet (mosaic)
A three-line statement at 22px, 30ch wide, sits at the 66% column with 64px of space below it; it is the only large text. Then a dense masonry of about twenty tiles in six columns with 4px gaps and 12px outer margins, built with CSS `columns: 6; column-gap: 4px` and `break-inside: avoid` on each figure. Tile sizes range from 90px squares to one feature tile of 280 by 160px that sits above the first two columns, outside the column flow. Images keep their natural ratios, so column bottoms are ragged. Every tile has one caption line at 12px directly beneath, 6px below the image and 14px above the next tile, in the form name, comma, type. Mix photographs, posters, details and textures. No hover chrome; the sheet reads at a glance and fills the full width.
On a phone: Three columns with 4px gaps and 10px margins; the feature tile spans the full width at the top. Captions stay at 12px but shorten to the name only. The statement sits above at 19px, full width and left-aligned.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

### 2. list — Two-ended rows, narrow column (list)
A list in one narrow column, 520px wide, starting at 22% of the viewport width or centred. Every row is 34–38px tall with no box and nothing drawn between rows. Each row has two ends: at the left, a 16px round image (a face, a logo, a colour dot) followed after 8px by the name at 15–16px in full ink; at the far right of the column, a short value at 13px in the soft ink (a date, a price, a domain, an opening time) with tabular figures. Entries that need a sentence run to two lines at the left, and the value aligns with the first line. Groups are separated by 40px of space and begin with one plain word in the soft ink at the same size. A heading of two to four words at 24–28px in medium weight sits 32px above the first row. Holds 10–40 rows.
On a phone: The column fills the width with 20px margins. Rows are 44px tall for touch; the left image stays 16px. Long values wrap beneath the name at 13px, left-aligned with it, and the row grows. Group spacing is 32px.

## 8. Motion — Index with preview
Rows of text are the navigation through the work; pointing at a row brings up its image in one fixed frame and dims the other rows. On touch screens each row shows its image inline instead. Fast and exact (about 240ms crossfade), no lag behind the pointer. Recipe: 'Index with preview, anchored to the row' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Density and order. The mass of small images forms a texture; the strict rows keep it calm.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
With fewer than about 20 images it looks unfinished; choose another direction. Do not add a big headline above the sheet. On a phone the frames become two per row at a fixed height and each run scrolls sideways. Rows that run past the right edge read as clipped in screenshots and to most visitors: wrap the frames onto the next line, or end each row inside the grid.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
